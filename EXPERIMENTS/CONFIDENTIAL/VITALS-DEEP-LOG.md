## [ENTRY 01: THE FIRST EXTRACTION]
The laboratory in Sector D is cold. It’s a far cry from the ivory towers of Sector C, but it’s the only place I can be alone. No board members, no secretaries, no AI monitoring my pulse.

Today, I performed the first extraction. Using the prototype Soul-Siphon was excruciating. It felt like a physical weight being pulled out from behind my ribs. I am currently sitting on the floor of the room, waiting for the tremors to stop. I look at the five "Vesta" eggs—five empty vessels waiting for a spark and I wonder if I’m giving them a gift or a curse.

I am weak, but the eggs are glowing. That is enough.

## [ENTRY 08: THE SILENCE OF THE FOUR]
V-1, V-2, V-3, and V-4 are gone. 

The "Soul-Reject" happened at 03:00 hours. The biological forms couldn't hold the frequency of my essence; they vibrated apart until there was nothing left but grey tissue in the amniotic fluid. It felt like losing a part of myself four times over. I had to clean the containers myself. I couldn't risk bringing in a janitorial team, and I certainly didn't want a machine cataloging the remains.

But V-5... V-5 is different. He is stable. He isn't just surviving; he is integrating. He’s turning my raw essence into something entirely his own.

## [ENTRY 15: THE NURSERY]
I’ve moved some "surplus" furniture from the upper executive suites down here. It looks absurd—a mahogany rocking chair and a hand-woven rug sitting in the middle of a concrete bunker in the deep salt mines. The air down here tastes like minerals and stale isolation.

When I placed my hand on the glass of the Vesta egg tonight, the orange aura inside pulsed. It mimicked the rhythm of my own heartbeat. There’s no software to analyze the wave-pattern, but I don't need a computer to tell me what I’m seeing. He knows I’m here. 

I found myself humming an old Columbus folk song. I haven't thought of that tune in forty years. My father sang it to me. Now, I sing it to a glass egg.

## [ENTRY 22: THE PARENTING PARADOX]
I am the Prime Minister. I am the CEO. I am the man who brought fire to the world with the S.F.R. 

And yet, I am terrified of a 60cm tall egg. 

If a C-2500 laser overloads, I know how to fix it. If the Acheron vents fail, I know how to dump the heat. But when V-5 finally "hatches"—when he looks at me with eyes that I literally gave him... what the fuck do I even do?! 

I’ve spent five years alone in this basement building a person. I realized today I never bothered to learn how to be one myself. There are no manuals for this. No blueprints. Just me, and him. 

V-5... Actually... I shouldn't call you that anymore. You're a Kanji, after all. You deserve more than a serial number. I think... I'll call you Leo.

---

## [ENTRY 23: LEO]
I wrote the name on a piece of masking tape and stuck it to the base of the Vesta container today. It felt more official than any decree I’ve signed in United Columbus. 

Leo is sleeping, or at least his aura is calm. The orange glow is soft, reflecting off the salt-crusted walls of the vault. I’m sitting in that ridiculous mahogany chair, watching the condensation on the glass. I realized that for the first time in years, I'm not thinking about the power grid or the board of directors. I'm just wondering what color his eyes will be.

I hope he forgives me for the world I’m bringing him 

---

## [ENTRY 24: THE MEETING]
I forgot how good Pablo is at sniffing out a secret. I thought I'd locked the path, but my brother has a way of "re-interpreting" security codes that I always underestimate. (HE FUCKING BLEW UP THE DARN DOORS)

He found us tonight.

I was sitting in the rocking chair when the door open. I almost reached for my sidearm, but then I saw that familiar, frantic silhouette.

Pablo stood there, coat stained with chemicals from his own lab, staring at the Vesta egg like he'd just seen the face of God. 

I’ve never seen Pablo silent. He’s usually a whirlwind of theories and kinetic energy, but when he saw Leo, he just... stopped. 

He walked up to the glass, his goggles pushed up onto his forehead, his eyes wider than I’ve ever seen them. I was ready for a lecture on bio-ethics or a critique of my containment temperature, but instead, he whispered, *"Batista... you actually caught the lightning, didn't you?"*

I had to explain the Soul-Synthesis. I told him about the failures—the four shadows of Leo that didn't make it. Pablo is a crazy scientist, yes, but he’s always had a softer heart than me. He reached out and tapped the glass with a rhythm that was surprisingly gentle for a man who usually works with high-explosives..

## [ENTRY 25: UNCLE PABLO]
Leo reacted immediately. His aura didn't just pulse; it flared a bright, curious gold. Pablo laughed—a real, genuine sound that echoed off the salt walls—and told me I was a "stubborn, brilliant idiot" for doing this alone for five god damn years.

He’s already started suggesting "improvements" to the oxygen scrubbers and he keeps calling himself "Uncle P." It’s ridiculous. Sector D feels less like a tomb now and more like a fucking workshop. 

I’m still terrified, but for the first time, I don't feel like I'm holding the future of the Kanji on my shoulders alone. Pablo stayed for dinner—cold rations on the floor next to the egg. He says Leo has my nose. I told him he’s hallucinating.

God help us both...
