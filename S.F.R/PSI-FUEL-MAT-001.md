The **[REDACTED] Series** consists of heavy-element synthetic compounds engineered specifically for **Project Synthetic**. These materials are characterized by their "Meta-Stable" atomic structure, allowing for high-yield fusion under Inertial Confinement. ## 3. ISOTOPE VARIANTS & SPECIFICATIONS

Parafield has developed three primary grades of the [REDACTED] compound to suit different operational requirements at Meridian Depthworks.

### 3.1 [REDACTED]-9: THE "STANDARD" COMPOUND
* **Classification:** GENERIC / STABLE
* **Usage:** 85% of standard operations.
* **Characteristics:** Offers a balanced ratio of energy output to cooling requirements. It is the most "predictable" isotope, with a steady burn rate and minimal fission-decay risk below 35 $keV$.
* **Longevity:** 24-hour continuous cycle before cell depletion.

### 3.2 [REDACTED]-8: THE "REACTIVE" COMPOUND
* **Classification:** HIGH-YIELD / VOLATILE
* **Usage:** Peak load demands or "Overclock" testing.
* **Characteristics:** Features a loosened atomic lattice that reacts violently to laser bombardment. It produces roughly **140% more energy** than the -9 variant. 
* **Cost:** Extremely high fuel consumption rate. The cell undergoes "Rapid Burn," depleting in approximately 4–6 hours.
* **Risk:** High susceptibility to **Thermal Runaway**. The margin of error for the Hex-Triad symmetry is reduced by 50%.

### 3.3 [REDACTED]-7: THE "EFFICIENCY" COMPOUND
* **Classification:** LOW-EMISSION / LONG-LIFE
* **Usage:** Maintenance cycles or low-power facility idling.
* **Characteristics:** A densified version of the compound with a higher "Ignition Threshold." It produces a cooler plasma, placing significantly less stress on the S.F.R. Superstructure walls.
* **Longevity:** Can remain active for up to **168 hours (1 week)** without replacement.
* **Constraint:** Cannot achieve high-energy states required for heavy industrial output.

## 4. THE "SHATTER" POINT (FISSION TRANSITION)
All isotopes in the [REDACTED] series share a common danger: **The Shatter Point**. 

If the internal kinetic energy exceeds **41.2 $keV$**, the heavy nuclei in the fuel cells stop fusing and begin to **Fission (Shatter)**. This creates a feedback loop where the fission energy drives more fusion, which in turn causes more fission. This results in the **"Golden Glow"** — a visible fission-luminescence event first recorded during the **1987 Thermal Runaway**, when primary SCRAM failure allowed the STI to reach 10.0 before manual ejection could be performed.
